How the model works

Every forecast shows what drove it.

The trained model estimates 24-hour water demand from root-zone moisture, crop stage, evapotranspiration and expected rainfall. Open a zone’s explanation to see how those inputs shaped its forecast.

Screen field readings

Convert volumetric moisture and root depth into a root-zone deficit. Rill flags readings older than 24 hours or outside the model’s training range so your team knows which zones to check.

Estimate zone demand

The model learns how soil deficit, crop-stage water use and rainfall affect net depth. Zone area and application efficiency convert that depth into the volume the pump would need to deliver.

Allocate available water

Suggested volumes go to higher-demand zones first by predicted depth. The plan caps total delivery at the available water or pump flow × hours, whichever is lower.

Model evaluation

Rill trained the model on 1,536 simulated zone observations and tested it on 384 observations from later dates in the same simulation. Farm pilots will measure accuracy against field observations.
